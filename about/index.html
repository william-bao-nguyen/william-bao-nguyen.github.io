Research overview

My path into plasma physics began at Imperial College London, where I worked on laser-wakefield-accelerator lab automation and learned to connect experimental diagnostics with computational models.

At SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory, I benchmarked quantum-physics modules in WarpX for multi-TeV linear-collider simulations. During my exchange year at MIT, I studied ion-energy modulations observed by the Magnetic Recoil Spectrometer at the National Ignition Facility.

I later developed start-to-end neutral-beam-injection simulations at Realta Fusion and worked in Simulation & Synthetic Diagnostics at Pacific Fusion. Across these projects, I have become especially interested in particle-in-cell simulation, scientific inference, and reliable research software.
