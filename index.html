Research overview

Bao's journey into plasma physics started at Imperial College London, where he worked on laser wakefield accelerator lab automation under Professor Zulfikar Najmudin. It was here that he was first introduced to different plasma diagnostics and particle-in-cell (PIC) codes, which he later worked on in several other projects!

In the summer of his second year, he did a SULI internship at SLAC National Lab under Professor Spencer Gessner. He worked on benchmarking the PIC code WarpX for linear collider applications.

During his exchange year at MIT, Bao learned more about the milestone ignition achievement at NIF and started to develop an interest in fusion energy. Here, he worked under Dr. Maria Gatu Johnson at the High Energy Density Physics Division to simulate modulated deuteron spectra observed by the MRS - one of the group's most important diagnostics.

Last summer, Bao worked at Realta Fusion under Dr. Kai Shih to develop PIC code WarpX for start-to-end simulation of neutral-beam-injection (NBI) devices. Outside of plasma physics, Bao briefly worked on computational chemistry at the University of Medicine & Pharmacy HCMC (in Vietnam). Here, he ran molecular dynamics simulation to evaluate the potential of different molecules in preventing antibiotics resistance in different bacteria.

Simulations of modulated deuteron spectra observed by the MRS

Outside of my interest in plasma accelerators, I was also intrigued by fusion energy, especially with the famous achievement of ignition at the National Ignition Facility around this time. Therefore in my exchange year at MIT, I worked at the High Energy Density Physics group at the MIT PSFC under the guidance of Dr. Maria Gatu Johnson. My project involves using the particle-in-cell code EPOCH to investigate the origin of unexpected ion energy modulations observed by the Magnetic Recoil Spectrometer (MRS) at the National Ignition Facility (NIF). This is important because the MRS diagnoses the key implosion parameters at NIF, and modulations can affect these inferred values.

Schematic of the MRS. Picture taken from J.A. Frenje et al., Phys. Plasmas 16, 042704 (2009).

Beam-beam effects in linear colliders

In the summer of 2023, I worked as a SULI intern at SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory under the guidance of Professor Spencer Gessner. As part of the multi-TeV beam-beam working group, I focused on validating and benchmarking quantum physics packages in the particle-in-cell (PIC) code WarpX for linear collider applications. This work addressed critical challenges such as energy losses from quantum effects like beamstrahlung, which can significantly impact collider performance at TeV scales.

This was my first experience running large-scale simulations on supercomputers! My findings demonstrated that WarpX's quantum physics packages not only produce results consistent with the established beam-beam code GUINEA-PIG but also scale more efficiently for large-scale problems.

Automation of a laser wakefield accelerator

In the summer of my first year, I read about plasma accelerators and how they accelerate particles over significantly shorter distances compared to conventional accelerators. I reached out to Professor Zulfikar Najmudin in the laser-plasma group at Imperial College London for a summer project. This was my first exposure to plasma physics, and I learned much about what physics research would entail! My project involved creating a control system with Python to automate gas jet experiments and analysis in the lab. Besides my main project, I also worked with another summer intern to model and characterize a pulsed magnet using B dot probes and a Rogwoski coil. Based on these measurements, we proposed an improved design for the magnet to reach the target B-field strength.

I very much enjoyed my time with the lab. In my second year, I continued working part-time, assisting with interferometry set-ups, and automating phase-retrieval and tomography codes. It was here that I eventually learned about PIC code (and so it would begin my 'PIC code career' in the next few years)!

Modeling Adaptative Optics with Object Orientated Programming

As part of my studies at Imperial, I worked on an Object Orientated Programming Python project. Given my previous experience with optics and interferometry, I decided to work on a ray tracer project, where I model how a light ray would get travel through different medium depending on their refractive indices and curvatures.

As an extension to this project, I implemented 'adaptive optics' / aspheric surfaces to improve the final image quality. This is a technique used in astronomy, which eliminates the blurriness of images.

You can find my project's code on GitHub and my report here!

Molecular Dynamics Simulations

At the University of Medicine & Pharmacy HCMC in Vietnam, I worked on a computational chemistry project under Professor Khac-Thai Minh. Efflux pump is a protein complex that allows bacteria to remove toxins, which can contribute to dangerous antibiotic resistance. My project involved using Molecular Dynamics simulation (GROMACs) to study different molecules that can act as inhibitors to a particular efflux pump in certain bacteria. Personally, this was a very interesting cross-disciplinary experience and it was also one of my first exposures to computational research!

My very first DPP experience

This past week, I attended my first APS Division of Plasma Physics (DPP) conference in Atlanta, and it was an unforgettable experience. As the largest plasma physics conference in the U.S., it brought together an incredible community of researchers, students, and professionals.

This year, I presented a poster on my work exploring modulated deuteron spectra observed by the Magnetic Recoil Spectrometer (MRS). I had many positive interactions, with discussions sparking new ideas and directions for my work. It was also exciting to finally meet several collaborators in person after months of remote communication.

A photo of me at the poster session

Beyond my poster session, I thoroughly enjoyed the talks on fundamental plasma physics and the progress being made toward inertial fusion energy. These sessions provided fresh insights and helped contextualize the significance of our collective efforts in the field.

Overall, it was a very positive experience at DPP this year and I am already looking forward to next year (hopefully giving a talk when my work is more complete!).
